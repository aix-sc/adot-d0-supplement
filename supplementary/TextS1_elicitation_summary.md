# Text S1 — Structured questionnaire answered by the table's author

**Respondent:** Arata Fujiwara (AF), author of the Asian Distance Optimization Table (ADOT). **Instrument:** structured questionnaire, version 4 (Japanese), eight sections. The section on expected level dependence (C) was answered before the section presenting results from this study (H). **Date and mode:** answered in writing on 7 September 2026, by the respondent alone, without recording; recorded by the respondent. Responses are paraphrased; the original Japanese form is available from the corresponding author.

## Provenance of the table (section A)

- The four inter-distance multipliers came from AF's own competitive records, from the records of athletes he coached, and from long-standing intuition rather than calculation. The largest single source was the records of other athletes and rivals. Approximately 300 athletes have been observed over his career.
- Partial records and notes survive and may be referenced.
- The reference point (level 78 = 3,000 m in 8:00) was chosen as a round number.
- The level scalar was applied by multiplication, with each step made smaller as ability increases because one second matters more at higher speed. The memo values (1.0165 to 1.006) and the values recomputed from the table (1.025 to 1.004) both express this intent; neither should be read as a precise specification.
- The table reflects the period from 2020 onward. AF's view is that carbon-plated shoes have made marathon and half-marathon times easier to predict from shorter distances.
- The left-hand index (30–85) is a number chosen to resemble VO2max values; it is not Daniels' VDOT. AF attributes the mismatch at the top of the range (a 13:16 5,000 m runner listed at 85, which is about 80 on Daniels' scale) to differences in running economy between Japanese and Western runners.
- Level 78 was set first and the multipliers between events were then fixed from it.

## Intended range (section B)

- Intended users: corporate-team and university ekiden runners, and club runners around 2:30; partly applicable to sub-3 and sub-4 runners; not intended for first-time finishers.
- Range of confidence: around level 78.
- Experience with runners at level 30 (about 4:43): almost none.
- AF expressed concern about applying the table outside his experience and wished to have it examined.

## Expected level dependence, answered before seeing results (section C)

- Whether the 5,000 m to marathon ratio is the same for a 13:40 runner and a 30:00 runner: slightly different; recreational runners vary more.
- Which group falls further short of its 5,000 m ability: undecided; if both have trained fully, elite runners may fade more because their race pace is set for them and may not be their optimal pace. Over-pacing by recreational runners was not considered.
- Predictions given as times (5,000 m → marathon): 13:40 → 2:08–2:10; 15:30 → 2:25; 19:00 → 2:55; 25:00 → 4:30; 30:00 → barely finishing. These imply ratios of 9.2–9.4 up to 19:00 and 10.8 at 25:00.
- Whether the half-to-full ratio changes with ability: unsure; either is possible.

## Assumed conditions (section D)

- Training assumed: recreational runners at least one run of 25 km or more; elite runners about 200 km per week for three months.
- First marathons: usable, but errors can be large if training is insufficient.
- The 5,000 m time to use: best within the previous year.
- Weather: heat has a large effect.
- Track-oriented and marathon-oriented athletes convert differently; individual characteristics vary widely.

## Expectations before validation (section E)

- A 5,000 m time is an "entry ticket"; the outcome depends on how much marathon training follows it. Errors will mostly fall on the slow side, because marathon training takes time that many runners do not complete.
- No experience with Indonesian or Thai runners; differences between populations are possible, as are differences due to heat; the outcome under equal training is unknown.

## Revision criteria (section F)

- The table should be revised if practitioners report that it is wrong, or if properly collected statistics disagree with it; it is confirmed if both agree.

## Publication (section G)

- The statement that the ratios derive from AF's practical knowledge may be made, on the condition that the manuscript states they were not derived statistically. AF participates as a co-author, permits the source records to be referenced, and asked to review the Methods text before submission.

## Reaction to the results (section H)

- On being shown the comparison with VDOT: in his experience Japanese runners tend to be relatively stronger at longer distances; the constant ratio matches his sense.
- On the results of this study: the outcome depends largely on race-day pacing; over-paced runners fade in most cases regardless of level, elite runners perhaps less; the equivalence assumes an appropriate pace, which most runners do not keep.
